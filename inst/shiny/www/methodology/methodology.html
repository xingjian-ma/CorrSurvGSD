Closed-Form Methodology for Correlated Group Sequential Designs

Inputs and notation

Sample size and analysis looks

  • \(n_T\): treatment-group sample size.
  • \(n_C\): control-group sample size.

\[ n=n_T+n_C,\qquad r=\frac{n_T}{n_C}. \]

  • \(n\): total sample size, and \(r\): treatment-to-control allocation ratio.
  • \(d_\ell^{PFS}\): target PFS event count at analysis look \(\ell\).
  • \(\ell=1,\ldots,L\): the ordered analysis looks; the target event counts are strictly increasing.

Survival and accrual inputs

  • \(\operatorname{median}_{PFS,C}\): control-group median PFS.
  • \(\operatorname{median}_{OS,C}\): control-group median OS.
  • \((HR_{PFS},HR_{OS})\): treatment-to-control hazard ratios when the hazard-ratio input mode is selected.
  • \((\operatorname{median}_{PFS,T},\operatorname{median}_{OS,T})\): treatment-group medians when the median-survival input mode is selected.
  • \(v_i\): accrual rate on segment \(i\).
  • \((t_{i-1},t_i]\): calendar interval for accrual segment \(i\).
  • \(R_i=t_i-t_{i-1}\): duration of accrual segment \(i\).
  • \(U\): enrollment time, with density \(v_i/n\) on segment \(i\) and segment probability \(v_iR_i/n\).

Sequential testing inputs

  • \(A_\ell\): calendar cutoff corresponding to look \(\ell\).
  • \(d_\ell^{OS}\): expected OS event count at cutoff \(A_\ell\), derived from the PFS event target.
  • \(a_\ell^k\): efficacy boundary for endpoint \(k\) at look \(\ell\).
  • \(c_\ell^k\): futility boundary for endpoint \(k\) at look \(\ell\); futility looks are restricted to pre-final looks.
  • \(\alpha\): one-sided Type I error level.
  • \(S_k\in\{\mathrm{OF},\mathrm{Pocock},\mathrm{HSD}\}\): alpha-spending family selected for endpoint \(k\).
  • \(\gamma_k\): HSD gamma parameter for endpoint \(k\) when \(S_k=\mathrm{HSD}\).
  • \((\mathrm{primary},\mathrm{secondary})\in\{(PFS,OS),(OS,PFS)\}\): fixed gatekeeping order.

Computation and optional simulation

  • \(\mathrm{integration\_seed}\): seed for theoretical multivariate-normal integration.
  • \(\mathrm{simulation\_seed}\): seed for optional Monte Carlo simulation.
  • \(n_{sim}\): requested number of Monte Carlo replicates when simulation is enabled.

Fleischer model and analysis timing

For treatment or control arm \(g\), the Fleischer model uses two independent latent event times: time to progression (TTP) and time to death (OS).

\[ T_g^{TTP}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda_{1,g}),\qquad T_g^{OS}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda_{2,g}),\qquad T_g^{TTP}\perp T_g^{OS}. \]

  • \(T_g^{TTP}\) is time to progression and \(T_g^{OS}\) is the OS event time, which is the time to death.
  • The two latent times are independent within an arm, while PFS and OS remain statistically related because PFS includes the same OS event time.

The observed endpoint times and their hazards are

\[ T_g^{PFS}=\min(T_g^{TTP},T_g^{OS}),\qquad \Lambda_g=\lambda_{1,g}+\lambda_{2,g}. \]

  • \(\Lambda_g\) is the PFS hazard, and \(\lambda_{2,g}\) is the OS hazard.
  • For the control arm, the supplied medians imply the latent hazards through

\[ \Lambda_C=\frac{\log(2)}{\operatorname{median}_{PFS,C}}, \qquad \lambda_{2,C}=\frac{\log(2)}{\operatorname{median}_{OS,C}}, \qquad \lambda_{1,C}=\Lambda_C-\lambda_{2,C}. \]

  • The treatment arm can be parameterized directly by medians, or by hazard ratios using

\[ \Lambda_T=HR_{PFS}\Lambda_C,\qquad \lambda_{2,T}=HR_{OS}\lambda_{2,C},\qquad \lambda_{1,T}=\Lambda_T-\lambda_{2,T}. \]

  • The validator requires the resulting latent hazards to be positive and rejects incomplete or conflicting treatment-effect specifications.

For a subject enrolled at time \(u\) and observed at calendar cutoff \(A\), write the available follow-up as \(a=A-u\) for \(u\le A\).

\[ a=A-u,\qquad \delta^k(A)=\mathbf{1}\{T^k\le a\},\qquad \widetilde T^k(A)=\min\{T^k,a\}. \]

  • \(a=A-u\) is the available follow-up; subjects enrolled after \(A\) are outside the integration domain and contribute no follow-up.
  • \(\delta^k(A)\) records whether endpoint \(k\) has been observed by the cutoff, and \(\widetilde T^k(A)\) is the administratively truncated time used in the moment calculations.

The PFS target determines each calendar cutoff by matching expected PFS events to the requested event count.

\[ n_T\,m_{\delta_\ell^{PFS}}^{(T)}(A_\ell) +n_C\,m_{\delta_\ell^{PFS}}^{(C)}(A_\ell) =d_\ell^{PFS}. \]

  • \(m_{\delta_\ell^{PFS}}^{(g)}(A_\ell)\) is the expected PFS event indicator for one subject in arm \(g\) at cutoff \(A_\ell\).
  • The application solves this monotone equation for \(A_\ell\) in increasing order, so the result is a calendar analysis schedule rather than an assumed fixed time schedule.

The same calendar cutoffs define the OS information used at each look.

\[ d_\ell^{OS} =n_T\,m_{\delta_\ell^{OS}}^{(T)}(A_\ell) +n_C\,m_{\delta_\ell^{OS}}^{(C)}(A_\ell). \]

  • \(d_\ell^{OS}\) is an expected event count derived from the PFS-driven cutoff, not an additional user-specified target.
  • This coupling allows PFS and OS analyses to use different efficacy or futility look selections while remaining tied to the same calendar schedule.

Closed-form moments and joint correlation

For endpoint \(k\), arm \(g\), and look \(\ell\), collect the event indicator and truncated follow-up time into the per-subject vector

\[ \mathbf{X}_{g,k,\ell} =\begin{pmatrix} \delta^k_\ell\\ \widetilde T^k_\ell \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{m}_{g,k,\ell}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{g,k,\ell}], \qquad \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{g,k,\ell} =\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{X}_{g,k,\ell}). \]

  • The vector \(\mathbf{m}_{g,k,\ell}\) supplies the single-look means required for event counts and log-hazard-ratio variance calculations.
  • The covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{g,k,\ell}\) is obtained by evaluating the exponential expectations over the piecewise-uniform enrollment distribution.
  • For PFS look \(\ell_1\) and OS look \(\ell_2\), combine the two endpoint vectors into the four-dimensional vector

\[ \mathbf{X}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2} =\begin{pmatrix} \delta^{PFS}_{\ell_1}\\ \widetilde T^{PFS}_{\ell_1}\\ \delta^{OS}_{\ell_2}\\ \widetilde T^{OS}_{\ell_2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{m}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2} =\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2}], \qquad \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2} =\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{X}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2}). \]

  • The covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2}\) contains the PFS single-look block, the OS single-look block, and the PFS/OS cross-look block.
  • The cross-look block is the upper-right \(2\times2\) block, denoted \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{PFS,OS}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2}\); it captures dependence from the shared latent OS event time and common enrollment timing.
  • Subtracting products of corresponding means converts each analytically evaluated product expectation into the covariance entries in \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2}\).

For each endpoint and look, the log-hazard-ratio approximation uses the map \(h(x_1,x_2)=\log(x_1)-\log(x_2)\). Its gradient at the arm-specific means is

\[ \mathbf{g}_{g,k,\ell} =\begin{pmatrix} 1/m_{\delta^k_\ell}^{(g)}\\ -1/m_{\widetilde T^k_\ell}^{(g)} \end{pmatrix}. \]

  • The vector \(\mathbf{g}_{g,k,\ell}\) translates variation in event counts and person-time into variation in the estimated log hazard ratio.
  • The Delta method then combines the treatment and control contributions.

The resulting marginal variance at endpoint \(k\), look \(\ell\), is

\[ \operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\theta}^k_\ell) =\sum_{g\in\{T,C\}} \frac{1}{n_g} \mathbf{g}_{g,k,\ell}^{\mathsf T} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{g,k,\ell} \mathbf{g}_{g,k,\ell}. \]

  • \(\widehat{\theta}^k_\ell\) is the estimated log hazard ratio at that endpoint and look.
  • The sample-size factors scale per-subject covariance to the two-arm estimator variance.

The cross-endpoint covariance between PFS look \(\ell_1\) and OS look \(\ell_2\) is

\[ \operatorname{Cov}(\widehat{\theta}^{PFS}_{\ell_1}, \widehat{\theta}^{OS}_{\ell_2}) =\sum_{g\in\{T,C\}} \frac{1}{n_g} \mathbf{g}_{g,PFS,\ell_1}^{\mathsf T} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{PFS,OS}_{g,\ell_1,\ell_2} \mathbf{g}_{g,OS,\ell_2}. \]

  • Standardizing this covariance by the two marginal standard deviations produces the PFS/OS correlations.
  • Within an endpoint, the canonical group-sequential correlation uses the information represented by expected events.

\[ \rho^k_{\ell_1,\ell_2} =\sqrt{ \frac{d^k_{\min(\ell_1,\ell_2)}} {d^k_{\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)}}}. \]

  • Combining the within-endpoint correlations with the standardized cross-endpoint covariances gives the joint correlation matrix for the multivariate normal vector.

\[ \mathbf{Z} =\left(Z_1^{PFS},\ldots,Z_L^{PFS},Z_1^{OS},\ldots,Z_L^{OS}\right)^{\mathsf T} \sim\mathcal{N}_{2L}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{R}). \]

  • \(\mathbf{Z}\) is the joint Z-score vector, \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\) is its mean-drift vector, and \(\mathbf{R}\) is its joint correlation matrix.
  • The ordering in both \(\mathbf{Z}\) and \(\mathbf{R}\) is PFS looks first and OS looks second, with one entry for each endpoint-look statistic.

Sequential boundaries and theoretical power

The assumed hazard ratio determines the mean Z-score drift at each endpoint and look.

\[ \theta^k_\ell =-\log(HR_k) \sqrt{\frac{d^k_\ell r}{(1+r)^2}}, \qquad r=\frac{n_T}{n_C}. \]

  • A treatment benefit with \(HR_k<1\) gives positive drift in the direction of the one-sided efficacy boundary.
  • PFS uses \(d_\ell^{PFS}\), while OS uses the derived \(d_\ell^{OS}\), so their drift vectors can differ even at the same calendar look.

The selected alpha-spending family determines the efficacy boundaries at the configured looks.

  • OF, Pocock, and HSD are selectable endpoint-specific spending families.
  • The one-sided alpha level controls the total efficacy spending, while HSD gamma controls how spending is distributed across looks.
  • Futility boundaries are created only at selected pre-final futility looks and are obtained from the corresponding futility hazard-ratio thresholds.
  • The boundary table displayed by Shiny reports both endpoints on Z, hazard-ratio, and one-sided p-value scales.

For information \(d^k_\ell\), define \(s^k_\ell\) as the information scale of the log-hazard-ratio statistic. A finite Z boundary converts as

\[ s^k_\ell =\sqrt{\frac{d^k_\ell r}{(1+r)^2}}, \qquad HR^k_\ell =\exp\left(-\frac{Z^k_\ell}{s^k_\ell}\right), \qquad p^k_\ell=1-\Phi(Z^k_\ell). \]

  • \(HR^k_\ell\) is the hazard-ratio threshold equivalent to the Z boundary, and \(p^k_\ell\) is its one-sided p-value.
  • Unconfigured boundaries remain infinite on the Z scale and are unavailable on the displayed HR and p scales.

For endpoint \(k\), the incremental marginal power at efficacy look \(\ell\) is the probability of first crossing there.

\[ \pi^k_\ell =\mathbb{P}\left( c^k_j\le Z^k_j<a^k_j\ \text{for all }j<\ell, \quad Z^k_\ell\ge a^k_\ell \right). \]

  • \(a^k_j\) is the efficacy boundary and \(c^k_j\) is the futility boundary, with infinite values representing an unconfigured boundary.
  • The probability is evaluated under the endpoint-specific mean drift and within-endpoint correlation matrix.
  • Summing the mutually exclusive incremental probabilities gives cumulative marginal power through look \(\ell\).

With a fixed testing hierarchy, let \(p\) denote the primary endpoint and \(s\) the secondary endpoint. The joint-power matrix has one cell for each primary look \(\ell_1\) and eligible secondary look \(\ell_2\ge\ell_1\).

\[ \operatorname{Power}_{joint} =\sum_{\ell_1=1}^{L}\sum_{\ell_2=\ell_1}^{L} \mathbb{P}\left( \left\{ \begin{aligned} &c^p_h\le Z^p_h\le a^p_h &&\text{for }h=1,\ldots,\ell_1-1,\\ &Z^p_{\ell_1}\ge a^p_{\ell_1} \end{aligned} \right\} \cap \left\{ \begin{aligned} &c^s_h\le Z^s_h &&\text{for }h=1,\ldots,\ell_1-1,\\ &c^s_h\le Z^s_h\le a^s_h &&\text{for }h=\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_2-1,\\ &Z^s_{\ell_2}\ge a^s_{\ell_2} \end{aligned} \right\} \right). \]

  • The first brace is the primary endpoint’s first efficacy crossing at look \(\ell_1\): every earlier look remains above its futility boundary and below its efficacy boundary.
  • The second brace is the secondary endpoint’s first eligible efficacy crossing at look \(\ell_2\): it avoids futility at every earlier look, remains below efficacy from the gate-opening look \(\ell_1\) through look \(\ell_2-1\), and crosses efficacy at look \(\ell_2\).
  • Before look \(\ell_1\), the secondary endpoint is not required to remain below an efficacy boundary because the hierarchy has not yet opened for it.
  • The displayed joint-power matrix records each crossing pair, and its cells sum to the reported hierarchical joint power.
  • The full PFS/OS joint correlation matrix and the two drift vectors define the multivariate normal probabilities used for these cells.

Simulation and reproducibility

  • Simulation is optional and is used to compare empirical Monte Carlo summaries with the closed-form results under the same Fleischer assumptions.
  • When enabled, the application reports simulated counterparts for marginal and joint power while retaining the theoretical boundary and power calculations.
  • The integration_seed controls reproducible multivariate-normal integration for the theoretical crossing probabilities.
  • The simulation_seed controls reproducible empirical summaries, and n_sim controls the requested number of Monte Carlo replicates.
  • The computational tolerance controls the numerical solution of the calendar-cutoff equations, while display precision affects presentation only.
  • The Shiny results can be reviewed as design summary, boundary, and power views, and the displayed result tables can be exported as CSV without changing the underlying calculations.
