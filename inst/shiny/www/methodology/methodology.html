Methodology for CorrSurvGSD

Input validation and design setup

Sample size

  • \(n_T\): treatment-group sample size.
  • \(n_C\): control-group sample size.
  • \(n\): total sample size with \(n=n_T+n_C\).
  • \(r\): treatment-to-control allocation ratio with \(r=\frac{n_T}{n_C}\).
  • \(d_\ell^{PFS}\): user-supplied PFS event target at look \(\ell\), where \(\ell=1,\ldots,L\) and \(L\) is the total number of looks. The validator requires every target to be a positive integer, requires the targets to be strictly increasing, and requires \(d_L^{PFS}\le n\).

Survival parameters and Fleischer model

We consider the Fleischer model for TTP (Time to Progression), PFS (Progression-Free Survival), and OS (Overall Survival) in a two-arm trial. The model is parameterized by the following inputs:

  • \(\operatorname{median}_{PFS,C}\): control-group median PFS.
  • \(\operatorname{median}_{OS,C}\): control-group median OS.
  • \((HR_{PFS},HR_{OS})\): treatment-to-control hazard ratios when the hazard-ratio input mode is selected.
  • \((\operatorname{median}_{PFS,T},\operatorname{median}_{OS,T})\): treatment-group medians when the median-survival input mode is selected.

Within arm \(g\in\{C,T\}\), the Fleischer model specifies the latent event times for subject \(i\) as

\[ T_i^{TTP}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda_{1,g}),\qquad T_i^{OS}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda_{2,g}),\qquad T_i^{TTP}\perp T_i^{OS}. \]

The observed PFS time is the minimum of the latent TTP and OS times:

\[ T_i^{PFS}=\min(T_i^{TTP},T_i^{OS}),\qquad \Lambda_g=\lambda_{1,g}+\lambda_{2,g}. \] For the control arm, the supplied medians imply the hazard parameters

\[ \Lambda_C=\frac{\log(2)}{\operatorname{median}_{PFS,C}}, \qquad \lambda_{2,C}=\frac{\log(2)}{\operatorname{median}_{OS,C}}, \qquad \lambda_{1,C}=\Lambda_C-\lambda_{2,C}. \]

The treatment arm can be calculated from medians similarly, or from hazard ratios:

\[ \Lambda_T=HR_{PFS}\Lambda_C,\qquad \lambda_{2,T}=HR_{OS}\lambda_{2,C},\qquad \lambda_{1,T}=\Lambda_T-\lambda_{2,T}. \]

The validator requires positive control medians with \(\operatorname{median}_{PFS,C}<\operatorname{median}_{OS,C}\) and either positive treatment medians with \(\operatorname{median}_{PFS,T}<\operatorname{median}_{OS,T}\) or positive hazard ratios with \(\Lambda_T>\lambda_{2,T}\).

Accrual schedule

The accrual schedule has \(s\) segments. Segment \(i\) spans \((t_{i-1},t_i]\), has accrual rate \(v_i\), and has duration \(R_i=t_i-t_{i-1}\), with \(t_0=0\). The supplied internal boundaries determine the segment durations as

\[ R_1=\frac{n}{v_1}, \qquad s=1. \]

\[ R_i=t_i-t_{i-1}, \qquad i=1,\ldots,s-1,\quad s>1. \]

\[ R_s=\frac{n-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}v_iR_i}{v_s}, \qquad s>1. \] The final duration \(R_s\) is implied rather than entered directly and the validator requires \(R_s>0\) and rejects a schedule whose internal boundaries or rates cannot enroll the requested sample.

For an enrollment time \(U_i\), the resulting piecewise-uniform density has \(p_i=v_i/n\) on segment \(i\):

\[ f_U(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_i \mathbf{1}\left(t_{i-1}<u\le t_i\right), \qquad \mathbb{P}\left(t_{i-1}<U\le t_i\right)=p_iR_i. \]

Sequential design parameters

  • \(\alpha\): one-sided Type I error level, with \(0<\alpha<1\).
  • efficacy alpha-spending family for endpoint \(k\).
  • arbitrary efficacy looks from 1 through \(L\).
  • arbitrary futility looks from 1 through \(L-1\) and arbitrary futility hazard ratio at each look.
  • fixed gatekeeping order: PFS -> OS or OS -> PFS.

Per-subject moments

At calendar cutoff \(A_\ell\), subject \(i\) has follow-up \(F_{i,\ell}=\max(0,A_\ell-U_i)\). For endpoint \(k\in\{PFS,OS\}\), the truncated event time and event indicator are

\[ \tilde T^k_{i,\ell}=\min(T_i^k,F_{i,\ell}), \qquad \delta^k_{i,\ell}=\mathbf{1}\left(T_i^k\le F_{i,\ell}\right). \]

  • The single-look quantities are \(\delta^k_{i,\ell}\), \(\tilde T^k_{i,\ell}\), \((\tilde T^k_{i,\ell})^2\), and \(\delta^k_{i,\ell}\tilde T^k_{i,\ell}\).
  • The PFS/OS cross-look quantities are the four products formed from \(\delta^{PFS}_{i,\ell_1}\), \(\tilde T^{PFS}_{i,\ell_1}\), \(\delta^{OS}_{i,\ell_2}\), and \(\tilde T^{OS}_{i,\ell_2}\).

For a per-subject quantity \(X\) in arm \(g\), write \(m_X^{(g)}=\mathbb{E}[X]\). Its conditional expectation is first evaluated at a fixed follow-up. PFS terms use \(\Lambda_g\), OS terms use \(\lambda_{2,g}\), and cross-look terms use \(F_{i,\ell_1}\) and \(F_{i,\ell_2}\). The resulting conditional expressions are integrated over enrollment time; their explicit primitives are not repeated here.

For a single-look quantity at cutoff \(A_\ell\), let \(B_\ell=\min(A_\ell,R)\) and let \(b\) be the segment containing \(B_\ell\). Piecewise-uniform accrual gives

\[ m_X^{(g)} =\sum_{i=1}^{b-1}p_i\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i} \mathbb{E}\left[X\mid F_{i,\ell}=A_\ell-u\right]\,\mathrm{d}u +p_b\int_{t_{b-1}}^{B_\ell} \mathbb{E}\left[X\mid F_{i,\ell}=A_\ell-u\right],\mathrm{d}u. \]

Cross-look means use the same integration rule with \(B=\min(A_{\ell_1},A_{\ell_2},R)\) and conditional follow-ups \(F_{i,\ell_1}=A_{\ell_1}-u\) and \(F_{i,\ell_2}=A_{\ell_2}-u\).

Per-subject covariances are obtained from the corresponding means:

\[ \sigma_{X,Y}^{(g)} =m_{XY}^{(g)}-m_X^{(g)}m_Y^{(g)}. \]

Calendar cutoffs and expected OS events

The PFS event targets determine the calendar cutoffs through the PFS event moment above.

\[ n_T\,m_{\delta_\ell^{PFS}}^{(T)} +n_C\,m_{\delta_\ell^{PFS}}^{(C)} =d_\ell^{PFS}, \qquad \ell=1,\ldots,L. \]

  • \(m_{\delta_\ell^{PFS}}^{(g)}\) is the one-subject PFS event probability in arm \(g\), evaluated at cutoff \(A_\ell\).
  • The left-hand side is monotone in \(A_\ell\). The implementation starts at the previous cutoff, expands the upper bracket until it reaches the target, and solves the equation to tolerance tol.
  • Because the PFS targets are strictly increasing, the resulting calendar schedule is ordered as \(0<A_1<\cdots<A_L\).

Once the PFS-driven calendar cutoffs are available, the same single-look moment calculation yields the expected OS event counts.

\[ d_\ell^{OS} =n_T\,m_{\delta_\ell^{OS}}^{(T)} +n_C\,m_{\delta_\ell^{OS}}^{(C)}. \]

  • \(d_\ell^{OS}\) is derived, not entered by the user.
  • PFS and OS therefore share calendar cutoffs while retaining endpoint-specific expected information for later boundary and power calculations.

Joint distribution

Delta-method variances and covariances

For endpoint \(k\) in arm \(g\), the hazard-rate estimator at look \(\ell\) is \(\widehat\lambda^k_{g,\ell}=D^k_{g,\ell}/S^k_{g,\ell}\). The marginal log-hazard-ratio estimator is

\[ \widehat\theta^k_\ell =\log\widehat\lambda^k_{T,\ell} -\log\widehat\lambda^k_{C,\ell}. \]

The Wald Z statistic is

\[ Z^k_\ell =-\frac{\widehat\theta^k_\ell} {\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta^k_\ell)}}. \]

  • \(D^k_{g,\ell}\) is the group-specific total event count and \(S^k_{g,\ell}\) is the corresponding total truncated event time.
  • The delta method supplies the variance and covariance approximations needed for the joint distribution of these Z statistics.

Cross-endpoint covariance

For the PFS/OS covariance at looks \((\ell_1,\ell_2)\) in arm \(g\), the report starts from the per-subject vector

\[ \mathbf{X}_i =\left( \delta^{PFS}_{i,\ell_1}, \tilde T^{PFS}_{i,\ell_1}, \delta^{OS}_{i,\ell_2}, \tilde T^{OS}_{i,\ell_2} \right)^{\mathsf T}. \]

Its mean vector is \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(g)}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_i]\) and its per-subject covariance matrix is \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{(g)}_{\ell_1,\ell_2}=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{X}_i)\). The multivariate central limit theorem and delta method are applied to the two log-rate maps

\[ g_1(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=\log(x_1)-\log(x_2), \qquad g_2(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=\log(x_3)-\log(x_4). \]

Their gradient vectors at \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(g)}\) are

\[ \boldsymbol{\nabla}g_1\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(g)}\right) =\begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{1}{m_{\delta^{PFS}_{\ell_1}}^{(g)}}\\ -\dfrac{1}{m_{\tilde T^{PFS}_{\ell_1}}^{(g)}}\\ 0\\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\nabla}g_2\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(g)}\right) =\begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 0\\ \dfrac{1}{m_{\delta^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}}\\ -\dfrac{1}{m_{\tilde T^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} \end{pmatrix}. \]

Consequently, the within-group cross-endpoint covariance is obtained as

\[ \operatorname{Cov}\left( g_1\left(\overline{\mathbf{X}}_{n_g}\right), g_2\left(\overline{\mathbf{X}}_{n_g}\right) \right) \approx\frac{1}{n_g} \boldsymbol{\nabla}g_1\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(g)}\right)^{\mathsf T} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{(g)}_{\ell_1,\ell_2} \boldsymbol{\nabla}g_2\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(g)}\right). \]

Expanding the cross-endpoint quadratic form gives

\[ \operatorname{Cov}\left( \log\widehat\lambda^{PFS}_{g,\ell_1}, \log\widehat\lambda^{OS}_{g,\ell_2} \right) \approx\frac{1}{n_g}\left[ \frac{\sigma_{\delta^{PFS}_{\ell_1},\delta^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} {m_{\delta^{PFS}_{\ell_1}}^{(g)}m_{\delta^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} -\frac{\sigma_{\delta^{PFS}_{\ell_1},\tilde T^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} {m_{\delta^{PFS}_{\ell_1}}^{(g)}m_{\tilde T^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} -\frac{\sigma_{\tilde T^{PFS}_{\ell_1},\delta^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} {m_{\tilde T^{PFS}_{\ell_1}}^{(g)}m_{\delta^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} +\frac{\sigma_{\tilde T^{PFS}_{\ell_1},\tilde T^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} {m_{\tilde T^{PFS}_{\ell_1}}^{(g)}m_{\tilde T^{OS}_{\ell_2}}^{(g)}} \right]. \]

Treatment and control subjects are independent, so

\[ \operatorname{Cov}\left( \widehat\theta^{PFS}_{\ell_1}, \widehat\theta^{OS}_{\ell_2} \right) =\sum_{g\in\{T,C\}} \operatorname{Cov}\left( \log\widehat\lambda^{PFS}_{g,\ell_1}, \log\widehat\lambda^{OS}_{g,\ell_2} \right). \]

Marginal variance

For the marginal variance at endpoint \(k\) and look \(\ell\), use the two-dimensional per-subject vector

\[ \mathbf{X}_i^k =\left( \delta^k_{i,\ell}, \tilde T^k_{i,\ell} \right)^{\mathsf T}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\mu}_{k,\ell}^{(g)} =\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{X}_i^k\right], \qquad \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k,\ell}^{(g)} =\operatorname{Cov}\left(\mathbf{X}_i^k\right). \]

With \(g(x_1,x_2)=\log(x_1)-\log(x_2)\), the gradient vector is

\[ \boldsymbol{\nabla}g\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k,\ell}^{(g)}\right) =\begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{1}{m_{\delta^k_\ell}^{(g)}}\\ -\dfrac{1}{m_{\tilde T^k_\ell}^{(g)}} \end{pmatrix}. \]

The corresponding delta-method quadratic form is

\[ \operatorname{Var}\left( \log\widehat\lambda^k_{g,\ell} \right) \approx\frac{1}{n_g} \boldsymbol{\nabla}g\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k,\ell}^{(g)}\right)^{\mathsf T} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k,\ell}^{(g)} \boldsymbol{\nabla}g\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k,\ell}^{(g)}\right). \]

Expanding this quadratic form gives

\[ \operatorname{Var}\left(\log\widehat\lambda^k_{g,\ell}\right) \approx\frac{1}{n_g}\left[ \frac{\left(\sigma_{\delta^k_\ell}^{(g)}\right)^2}{\left(m_{\delta^k_\ell}^{(g)}\right)^2} -\frac{2\sigma_{\delta^k_\ell,\tilde T^k_\ell}^{(g)}} {m_{\delta^k_\ell}^{(g)}m_{\tilde T^k_\ell}^{(g)}} +\frac{\left(\sigma_{\tilde T^k_\ell}^{(g)}\right)^2}{\left(m_{\tilde T^k_\ell}^{(g)}\right)^2} \right]. \]

The log-hazard-ratio variance is the sum of its independent treatment and control contributions:

\[ \operatorname{Var}\left(\widehat\theta^k_\ell\right) =\sum_{g\in\{T,C\}} \operatorname{Var}\left( \log\widehat\lambda^k_{g,\ell} \right). \]

Asmptotic distribution

Standardizing this covariance by the two marginal standard deviations produces the PFS/OS correlations.

\[ \operatorname{Corr}\left(Z^{PFS}_{\ell_1},Z^{OS}_{\ell_2}\right) =\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat{\theta}^{PFS}_{\ell_1}, \widehat{\theta}^{OS}_{\ell_2})} {\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\theta}^{PFS}_{\ell_1}) \operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\theta}^{OS}_{\ell_2})}}. \]

Within an endpoint, the canonical group-sequential correlation uses the information represented by expected events.

\[ \operatorname{Corr}\left(Z^k_{\ell_1},Z^k_{\ell_2}\right) =\sqrt{\frac{d^k_{\min(\ell_1,\ell_2)}}{d^k_{\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)}}}. \]

Combining the within-endpoint correlations with the standardized cross-endpoint covariances gives the covariance matrix of the Z vector. The delta method therefore gives the following multivariate-normal approximation:

\[ \mathbf{Z} =\left(Z_1^{PFS},\ldots,Z_L^{PFS},Z_1^{OS},\ldots,Z_L^{OS}\right)^{\mathsf T} \dot\sim\mathcal{N}_{2L}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}). \]

Random stopping times and Anscombe’s theorem

In an event-driven trial, the realized calendar time for look \(\ell\) is random:

\[ A_\ell =\inf\left\{t\ge0: \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\left(U_i+T_i^{PFS}\le t\right) =d_\ell^{PFS}\right\}. \]

At this random time, the stopped PFS event count equals \(d_\ell^{PFS}\) by construction. The individual event indicators are therefore not i.i.d. Bernoulli variables at the realized look time, so a fixed-time central limit theorem cannot be applied directly.

Anscombe’s theorem gives the required asymptotic bridge. If \(Y_n\xrightarrow{d}Y\) and a random index \(\tau(t)\) satisfies \(\tau(t)/t\xrightarrow{p}1\), then, under regularity conditions,

\[ Y_{\tau(t)}\xrightarrow{d}Y. \]

When the realized event-driven look time converges to the expected look time in probability, the stopped Z-scores converge in distribution to the same multivariate-normal limit as if the look time were fixed. Thus, the multivariate-normal distribution of \(\mathbf{Z}\) is an asymptotic approximation for the event-driven group sequential design, rather than an assertion that the stopped per-subject contributions remain i.i.d. in finite samples.

Closed-form boundaries and power

Mean Z-score drift and futility boundaries

The assumed hazard ratio determines the mean Z-score drift at each endpoint and look.

\[ \mu^k_\ell(HR_k) =-\log(HR_k) \sqrt{\frac{d^k_\ell r}{(1+r)^2}}, \qquad r=\frac{n_T}{n_C}. \]

  • A treatment benefit with \(HR_k<1\) gives positive drift in the direction of the one-sided efficacy boundary.
  • PFS uses \(d_\ell^{PFS}\), while OS uses the derived \(d_\ell^{OS}\), so their drift vectors can differ even at the same calendar look.

The mean-drift function constructs each selected Z-scale futility boundary by evaluating it at the corresponding futility hazard-ratio threshold:

\[ c_\ell^k =\mu_\ell^k\left(HR_{\mathrm{futility}}^k\right) =-\log\left(HR_{\mathrm{futility}}^k\right) \sqrt{\frac{d^k_\ell r}{(1+r)^2}}. \] - Unconfigured futility bounds are initialized as $-.

Efficacy boundaries

The selected alpha-spending family determines the efficacy boundaries at the configured looks.

  • For multiple efficacy looks, the implementation calls gsDesign::gsDesign() with one-sided test.type = 1, the configured alpha level, and information fractions \(d^k_{\ell}/d^k_L\) at the configured efficacy looks.
  • The selected family is passed to gsDesign::gsDesign() as gsDesign::sfLDOF for OF, gsDesign::sfLDPocock for Pocock, or gsDesign::sfHSD with the user-supplied gamma for HSD.
  • With one efficacy look, the boundary is \(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\).
  • Efficacy boundaries \(a_\ell^k\) are constructed directly on the Z scale from the selected alpha-spending design.
  • Unconfigured efficacy bounds are initialized as \(\infty\).

Boundary-scale conversion

For information \(d^k_\ell\), a finite Z boundary converts to its hazard-ratio threshold as

\[ HR^k_\ell =\exp\left( -\frac{Z^k_\ell}{\sqrt{d^k_\ell r/(1+r)^2}} \right). \]

The same finite Z boundary converts to a one-sided p-value as

\[ p^k_\ell=1-\Phi(Z^k_\ell). \]

Marginal power

For endpoint \(k\), the incremental marginal power at efficacy look \(\ell\) is the probability of first crossing there.

\[ \text{Incremental Power}^k_\ell =\mathbb{P}\left( \bigcap_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\left\{c_j^k<Z_j^k<a_j^k\right\} \text{ and }Z_\ell^k\ge a_\ell^k \right). \]

The total marginal at look \(\ell\) is the cumulative probability of crossing efficacy at or before that look.

\[ \text{Cumulative Power}^k_\ell =\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\text{Incremental Power}^k_j. \]

Joint power

For \(1\le i\le j\le L\), let \(E_{ij}\) denote the event that PFS first crosses efficacy at look \(i\), opening the OS gate, and OS first crosses efficacy at look \(j\).

\[ E_{ij} =\left(\bigcap_{h=1}^{i-1} \left\{c_h^{PFS}<Z_h^{PFS}<a_h^{PFS}\right\}\right) \cap\left\{Z_i^{PFS}\ge a_i^{PFS}\right\} \cap\left(\bigcap_{h=1}^{i-1} \left\{c_h^{OS}<Z_h^{OS}\right\}\right) \cap\left(\bigcap_{h=i}^{j-1} \left\{c_h^{OS}<Z_h^{OS}<a_h^{OS}\right\}\right) \cap\left\{Z_j^{OS}\ge a_j^{OS}\right\}. \]

The OS efficacy boundary is not evaluated before look \(i\), because the OS test is not activated until PFS rejects; the OS path must nevertheless remain above its futility boundary.

We have the joint power at look \((i,j)\) as

\[ \operatorname{JointPower}_{ij}=\mathbb{P}(E_{ij}), \qquad 1\le i\le j\le L, \]

and the total joint power across all look pairs as

\[ \operatorname{JointPower} =\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{j=i}^{L}\operatorname{JointPower}_{ij}. \]
